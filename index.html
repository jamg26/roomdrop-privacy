RoomDrop privacy policy

Effective 24 November 2026 · Version 1.0.0 (updated for version code 3) · Published at https://jamg26.github.io/roomdrop-privacy/

RoomDrop is made by JAMGSOFT (contact: jammmg26@gmail.com). It sends files you choose from your Android phone or tablet to your Android TV or Google TV on the same local network. This policy explains what RoomDrop handles, where it goes, and how to delete it.

The short version

  • Your files go directly from your phone to your TV over your own network. JAMGSOFT has no server, never receives your files, and keeps no copy.
  • Transfers are encrypted, and the phone confirms the TV's certificate, from the TV's QR code or its six-digit code, before it sends anything.
  • No ads, no analytics, no account and no tracking by JAMGSOFT.
  • Google Play handles the optional Pro purchase. The Google Play Billing library sends Google its own purchase and diagnostic data, described below.

What RoomDrop handles

Files you choose to send. On the phone, RoomDrop reads only the files you pick, either in Android's photo picker (Photos & videos) or in Android's file picker (Files). The photo picker gives RoomDrop access only to the photos and videos you select; RoomDrop doesn't ask for permission to read your photo library or your storage. It reads each file twice: once to calculate a SHA-256 checksum, and once to send it. It doesn't copy them, and it doesn't browse your storage. It keeps a reference to your current selection (the file's address, name, size and type) so the selection survives a restart; it releases that access when the files are sent or when you forget the selection. Access to items from the photo picker can end when the app restarts; RoomDrop then removes them from the selection and asks you to choose them again.

Files your TV receives. On the TV, received files are kept only in RoomDrop's own storage (its Inbox), under random file names, after their size and checksum match what the phone announced. Photos and videos open in RoomDrop's own viewer on the TV, which reads the file locally and sends nothing anywhere. Another app can open an Inbox file only when you choose Open or Open with another app, and then only that one file. Uninstalling RoomDrop from the TV deletes the Inbox.

Transfer history. Each device keeps a short local history of batches (device name, time, file names, sizes, and whether each file was verified), so it can show results and recover after an interruption.

The local network. While the TV's Receive screen is open, RoomDrop listens on the TV's local network address and announces itself on the local network (mDNS/DNS-SD) with the TV's device name, a port number and a random session number. Other devices on the same network can see that announcement. It stops when you leave Receive. The phone sends the TV a device name for the approval screen, and the TV also shows the phone's local network address there, so you can tell which device is asking. RoomDrop shows only these local addresses and the TV's port; it doesn't read or show your network's name or other network details, and it doesn't store the phone's address.

Pairing codes. The TV shows a QR code and a six-digit code. Each is random, lasts two minutes, pairs one phone once, and stays in memory only. The six-digit code is checked with a password-authenticated key exchange (J-PAKE) that also confirms the TV's certificate; after three wrong tries the TV shows a new code, and after nine wrong tries it stops offering a typed code until you press New code on the TV (the QR keeps working). Codes are never saved, logged or backed up, and the six-digit code itself never crosses the network.

Staying paired. After pairing, the phone stays connected to the TV while both apps are open, so you can send more batches without pairing again. Every batch still needs your approval on the TV. The pairing ends when either side taps Disconnect, when you leave either app or close Receive, or after about 10 minutes without sending. Nothing about the pairing is saved; the next time, you pair again.

The camera (optional). On the phone, the camera is used only to read the QR code on your TV. Camera frames are analysed in memory and discarded; nothing is recorded or saved. If you don't allow the camera, you can type the TV's six-digit code instead.

The Pro purchase. Google Play processes payment. RoomDrop keeps Google's signed purchase receipt on your phone, encrypted with an Android Keystore key and excluded from backups, and checks it on the device. JAMGSOFT never sees your payment details.

RoomDrop has no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting SDK and doesn't use the advertising ID. JAMGSOFT doesn't sell or share personal data.

Backups

RoomDrop opts out of Android's cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. Your Inbox, history, selection and purchase record stay on the device they're on.

Deleting your data

  • On the phone: Privacy → Clear send history, Forget selected files (this releases access; your original files aren't touched), and Clear cache. Uninstalling removes everything RoomDrop stored.
  • On the TV: Inbox → select a file → Delete, or Storage → Delete all Inbox files, Clear cache and Clear receive history. Uninstalling RoomDrop removes the Inbox and everything else it stored.
  • Pro purchase: Google keeps purchase records under its own policy. A refund or revocation through Google Play ends Pro on the phone; nothing else is deleted.
  • Support email: write to jammmg26@gmail.com to delete our correspondence. We keep support email only as long as needed to resolve the request.

Security

Transfers use TLS from Android's own security libraries, with a fresh certificate each time Receive opens. The phone accepts only the certificate whose full SHA-256 fingerprint is in the TV's QR code, or that the TV proved with the six-digit code, and the TV accepts files only after you approve each batch on screen. No system is perfectly secure; if you believe you've found a security problem, please email us.

Children

RoomDrop is a general utility not directed at children, and it doesn't knowingly collect personal data from anyone.
